The Room That's Ready

A short film by Eli Harrigan · 3 minutes 30 seconds

A man crashes alone on a rural road before dawn, and nobody sees it. His car, his watch, and a regional coordination system do. The film follows what happens in the hour before he reaches the trauma center, from the point of view of the information that gets there first.

It was made for Dr. Michael Rotondo's 2026 Scudder Oration on Trauma, a named lecture of the American College of Surgeons, where it played in the middle of his talk in front of about a thousand trauma surgeons. Nothing in it was photographed, and nobody speaks.

Sound on. Full screen if you can. The heartbeat is part of the story.

The car reports the crash. The watch on his wrist reports his body. A coordination center resolves his location and a drone lifts from its pad in the same instant. His record arrives with one word that matters: he is anticoagulated. The system predicts he will bleed and stages blood at the trauma center before any human has reached him. The drone finds him with a thermal camera. Medics arrive to a scene that is already lit, treat him in the ambulance while the trauma center watches the same numbers, and confirm the prediction. When the doors open, the room is ready.

The systems in the film, a regional coordination center, a car that reports its own crash, a record that travels ahead of the patient, are near-future versions of things that exist today. The film's argument is that the difference between now and then is integration: the same information reaching the dispatcher, the medics, and the trauma team at the same time, and the response beginning before anyone has made a phone call.

Made for Dr. Michael Rotondo, Professor of Surgery, University of Rochester Medical Center.
Directed, produced, and edited by Eli Harrigan.
Original score by Gregg Perea.
Clinical content and system names by Dr. Rotondo.
